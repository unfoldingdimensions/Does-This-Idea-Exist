# 02 — Competitor Profiles & Landscape Matrix

**Access date for every source below: 2026-09-14.**
This file profiles **all 59 players named in 01-competitive-landscape.md**, one block each, then gives a single competitive-landscape matrix, maturity/traction signals, an adjacent-but-not-competitor list, and a conclusion.

## Scope

This file profiles **every player named in `01-competitive-landscape.md`** — one block each — and then presents them in a single sourced comparison matrix. For each player it records what they build, who they sell to, how they price, how they position themselves, and the URL that evidences it. It does **not** rank players by quality, recommend a partner, or cover any product that was not named in `01`. Everything is public desk research: no signups, no paid data, no hands-on product testing. Claims that could not be directly observed are tagged `[ASSUMPTION]` or `[OPEN QUESTION]`.

**Reading the evidence fields:** "Evidence:" is a live page I read during this research (all fetched 2026-09-14). Where a home URL could not be directly observed in a search result, it is tagged `[ASSUMPTION]` (canonical domain inferred from third-party evidence). Prices are **list prices from public pages or third-party comparison sites**, not quotes; they move fast. `not published` means I found no public price.

## Conclusion

- **The crowded part is the two ends:** free crowd-sourced directories/review sites (Segment B, 9 players, consolidating into G2) and high-volume AI tool catalogs (Segment E, 5 players, tens of thousands of unverified listings). Both compete on **volume and SEO**, and both are structurally unable to answer "is this idea distinct?"
- **The second-crowded part is paid research:** Startup databases (C) and CI tools (F) are crowded but fenced off behind $49/mo–$100k/yr paywalls, so they are not substitutes for a free founder tool.
- **The thin part — and where IdeaExists sits — is the free, verified, founder-facing middle.** No player surfaced that combines (a) a maintained corpus, (b) liveness verification, and (c) human-stamped, never-deleted entries. Validators (Segment A) produce one-off scores with no persistent, verified corpus; graveyards (Startups.RIP, Loot Drop, Failory) track *death* but only for curated cohorts and without liveness checking.
- **The closest philosophical neighbour is the "startup graveyard + rebuild" cluster** — young, sparsely populated, and explicitly premised on *"another founder already validated there was demand."* That thesis mirrors IdeaExists' "what would be meaningfully different before you build" framing and is the most direct emerging competitor set.
- **Data quality is the unclaimed axis.** Every crowded segment competes on volume; none competes on verification. This is the single most defensible angle available.
- **The strongest competitor is not a product at all** — it is the free status-quo reflex (Google/ChatGPT/Perplexity). Positioning must win the "why not just ask ChatGPT?" question, and the verification/liveness story is the only credible answer available.
- **Caveats:** prices are list prices from third-party pages, several home URLs are canonical-domain `[ASSUMPTION]`s evidenced indirectly, and no feature claims were hands-on verified. See `[OPEN QUESTION]` list above for the traction gaps.
